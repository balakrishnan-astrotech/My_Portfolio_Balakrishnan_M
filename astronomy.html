astronomy journey

From curiosity to a direction

Astronomy started for me with a simple curiosity about the universe. Over time, that curiosity developed into something more deliberate: learning the science behind the objects I was interested in and finding practical ways to connect my technical education with space science.

the journey

How my interests developed

My path toward astronomy has not been a straight academic route. I am currently studying Electronics and Communication Engineering, while independently building the physics, mathematics, programming and astronomy knowledge I will need for my future studies.

where it started

Curiosity about astronomy

My interest in astronomy began with curiosity about space and the universe. Questions about stars, planets, galaxies and the physical processes happening beyond Earth gradually made me want to understand astronomy more scientifically rather than only as an area of fascination.

That interest became a long-term goal: to eventually study astronomy and astrophysics formally and work in the field.

academic foundation

Electronics & Communication Engineering

I chose a Diploma in Electronics and Communication Engineering at Sankar Polytechnic College. Although ECE is not the same field as astrophysics, it gave me a practical engineering foundation in electronics, circuits, systems, measurement and hardware.

I began to see electronics not as something separate from astronomy, but as a possible foundation for working with scientific instruments, sensors, signals and data-acquisition systems.

expanding beyond electronics

Learning programming

As I continued with ECE, I started learning programming to complement my electronics background. Python became an important part of that process, particularly for calculations, data handling, visualisation and scientific exploration.

I have also worked with technologies such as HTML, CSS and JavaScript while building astronomy-related web projects. This helped me discover that programming could connect my technical interests with both scientific data and science communication.

first astronomy project

Celestial Gallery

One of the ways I combined programming with astronomy was through Celestial Gallery, an astronomy web application developed using HTML, CSS and JavaScript.

The project was not simply about learning web development. It gave me a way to work with a subject I genuinely enjoy and experiment with presenting astronomical information in a structured and accessible form.

space technology

Def-Space Summer Internship

My interest in space technology eventually led to the Def-Space Summer Internship at the Bharat Space Education Research Centre (BSERC).

During the internship, I worked on a proof-of-concept solar tracking and receiving system. The project used an Arduino, LDR sensors, a servo motor and a solar panel to explore sensor-guided alignment of a receiver toward a light source.

The idea was inspired by the broader concept of Space-Based Solar Power. More importantly for me, the project showed how electronics, sensors, programming and mechanical movement can come together in a space-technology application.

working with astronomical data

Virgo Constellation Data Analysis

My interest then moved further toward the data side of astronomy. I worked on a small Virgo Constellation Data Analysis project using information derived from a Hubble Space Telescope image.

I created a small dataset and explored it using statistical analysis, Excel and Matplotlib. It was an early attempt to move from simply looking at astronomical images to treating astronomical information as data that can be measured, organised and analysed.

moving toward astrophysics

Nuclear Astrophysics

I wanted to understand the physics behind astronomical phenomena, so I began studying Nuclear Astrophysics through an NPTEL course from IIT Roorkee.

This introduced me to topics such as thermonuclear reactions, reaction rates, cross-sections, the Gamow window, the astrophysical S-factor, the proton-proton chain and the CNO cycle.

This was an important step for me because it moved my learning beyond general astronomy toward the physical processes that help explain how stars work and how elements are produced in stellar environments.

where I am now

Building the foundation

I am currently a second-year Diploma student in Electronics and Communication Engineering. At the same time, I am deliberately building knowledge beyond my diploma curriculum.

My current learning spans physics, mathematics, electronics, programming, astronomy, data analysis and astrophysics. I am trying to understand these subjects as connected parts of one larger scientific foundation rather than studying them independently.

long-term direction

Toward astronomy & astrophysics

My long-term goal is to continue from my engineering education into higher studies in physics and astrophysics, gradually moving closer to professional astronomy and astronomical research.

The path ahead will require a much stronger foundation in mathematics, physics, scientific computing and astrophysics. I am still at the beginning of that path, so my focus now is on building those foundations carefully and gaining experience through projects, experiments, independent study and scientific communication.

where I'm heading

Engineering is my starting point, not my destination

ECE has given me a practical way to understand the physical systems behind technology. Programming has added a computational side to that foundation. Space-technology projects have allowed me to apply those skills, while astronomy data analysis and nuclear astrophysics have started taking me closer to the scientific side of my interests.

I see these experiences as connected steps. I am not claiming to be an astronomer yet. I am building toward becoming one.
